Section 1
Evaluation Criteria

All AI tools and SaaS platforms are assessed against the following criteria, grouped by category. These criteria reflect TRCAS's PDPA obligations, the federated parish governance model, the sensitivity of Archdiocesan data, and the principles of Magnifica Humanitas on AI ethics.

C1 — Data Training & Use

  • Does the tool use your inputs/prompts to train its AI models?
  • Is the no-training guarantee contractual (in a DPA/MSA), or just a setting the vendor can change?
  • Does it apply by default, or only if the admin manually opts out?
  • Does "paid individual" plan protect you, or only enterprise/API tiers?
  • Can the vendor unilaterally change the training policy without notice?

C2 — Data Retention

  • How long does the vendor retain your prompts and outputs after use?
  • Is zero data retention (ZDR) available? At what tier?
  • What happens to data after deletion — is permanent deletion guaranteed?
  • Are there external legal hold obligations that override your deletion requests?
  • Is retention configurable by our organisation's admin?

C3 — Data Residency & Sovereignty

  • Where are servers physically located — are they in a Singapore-friendly jurisdiction?
  • Is Singapore data residency available, committed to, or configurable?
  • Is data subject to any foreign national intelligence laws that could override our DPA?
  • Can data be processed in a region TRCAS selects (Singapore preferred; US/EU acceptable with DPA)?
  • Does overseas transfer have adequate safeguards (DPA, SCCs, adequacy decisions) for PDPA compliance?

C4 — Human Review

  • Can vendor employees read your conversations or prompts?
  • Is human review permitted for "safety" or "quality improvement" purposes?
  • Can you contractually prevent human access to your data?
  • Are there mandatory disclosure obligations to governments or courts?
  • Is there an independent audit mechanism?

C5 — Governance & Admin Controls

  • Does the vendor provide a signed Data Processing Addendum (DPA)?
  • Is SSO / SAML integration available for domain-level access control?
  • Is SCIM available for automated user provisioning and deprovisioning?
  • Can admins enforce policies across all users (no shadow accounts)?
  • Is audit logging of usage available? Does it capture content or only metadata?
  • Is there a compliance API or eDiscovery support?

C6 — Security Certifications

  • SOC 2 Type II certification (verified, not claimed)
  • ISO 27001 certification
  • ISO 42001 (AI management systems — newer standard)
  • Encryption at rest (AES-256 or equivalent) and in transit (TLS 1.2+)
  • Any documented security incidents or breaches? What was the response?

C7 — PDPA & Singapore Compliance

  • Is there a valid Data Processing Addendum covering PDPA obligations?
  • Does the vendor support data subject rights (access, correction, deletion)?
  • Is data transfer to overseas servers covered by adequate contractual safeguards?
  • Has the vendor faced regulatory action in Singapore or APAC?
  • Can the vendor sign a PDPA-compliant data processing arrangement?

C8 — Sensitive Data Risk

  • Is the tool safe for sacramental records, pastoral counselling notes, donor data?
  • Is it safe for staff HR data and minor (youth ministry) personal data?
  • Does the AUP prohibit use with sensitive or special category data?
  • What is the risk if a user accidentally pastes sensitive data?
  • Are there data loss prevention (DLP) controls that can be configured?

C9 — Ethical & Mission Alignment

  • Does the tool's business model or political affiliation conflict with Catholic values?
  • Is the AI capable of generating harmful, inappropriate, or anti-faith content?
  • Does the vendor have documented AI ethics principles?
  • Alignment with Magnifica Humanitas — does it safeguard human dignity?
  • Does the tool enable autonomous AI actions without human oversight?

C10 — Practical Usability for TRCAS

  • Is non-profit licensing available at reduced cost?
  • Does it integrate with our existing stack (Google Workspace, M365)?
  • Is the tool usable by non-technical staff and volunteers?
  • Is there an admin console that AITO can manage centrally?
  • Is the tool already in use by staff without governance (shadow AI)?
How to Read the Assessment Each tool is rated APPROVED, CONDITIONAL (permitted with specific controls), or PROHIBITED for use with Archdiocesan data. CONDITIONAL tools may be used for low-sensitivity tasks only unless the specific conditions noted are met. No tool — regardless of approval status — should ever receive sacramental records, pastoral counselling notes, donor financial data, or youth/minor personal data without explicit AITO sign-off.

Important Notes, Caveats & Next Steps

Read this section before acting on any assessment in this document. It contains governance caveats, absolute data rules, recommended immediate actions, and questions to ask every vendor before procurement.

Verify Before You Procure

  • Vendor policies change frequently — sometimes without notice. Every certification claim, data residency commitment, and pricing figure should be verified directly with the vendor's current Trust Center before signing any contract.
  • This document reflects publicly available information as of 22 August 2026 (last full periodic reassessment). Where "verify with vendor" is noted in the assessment table, treat that as a prerequisite before procurement — not a suggestion.
  • Vendor Trust Centers: OpenAI · Google Workspace · Microsoft Copilot · Anthropic
Governance Caveats

C-A · Paying More ≠ More Privacy (Individual Tier)

  • ChatGPT Plus/Pro and Gemini Advanced (personal) privacy rules are nearly identical to the free tier — only Enterprise plans with a signed DPA provide contractual protection.
  • A paid personal subscription does NOT equal enterprise privacy. The only meaningful dividing line is the existence of a signed DPA — not the price.
  • Staff using personal paid accounts for work data are NOT protected, regardless of subscription level.

C-B · US CLOUD Act Exposure

  • All US-based providers (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Meta, xAI, Anthropic, GitHub) are subject to the US CLOUD Act and National Security Letters.
  • Enterprise DPAs mitigate but do not eliminate this exposure — a US court order can compel data disclosure regardless of your DPA.
  • For highly sensitive data (pastoral records, donor data, HR), the safest architecture remains on-premises or in a jurisdiction with strong data sovereignty laws.
  • This is a risk to document and accept consciously — not a reason to avoid all cloud AI, but a reason to never put the most sensitive data into any US-hosted tool.

C-C · Machine Unlearning Is Not Practically Possible

  • Deleting your data from a vendor's systems does not remove it from AI models already trained on it.
  • If sensitive data was used in a training run before opt-out was activated, it cannot be "untrained." The damage is done.
  • The only effective protection is opting out of training before data is processed — retroactive opt-out is meaningless for data already used in training.
  • This is why AITO must enforce opt-out at org level from day one, not after a breach or complaint.

C-D · Technical Access ≠ Legal Permission to Monitor

  • Even where admins can technically access employee prompts (eg. ChatGPT Enterprise Compliance API, Microsoft Purview), Singapore employment law, PDPA obligations, and any applicable works-council or employment contract requirements may restrict over-broad monitoring.
  • Technical capability is not legal permission. AITO should obtain legal advice before implementing any system that reads or analyses employee AI conversations.
  • Monitoring policy should be documented in employment contracts and the Acceptable Use Policy — with clear staff notice — before any monitoring is activated.

C-E · If Staff Are Already Using Prohibited Tools — What To Do

  • Stop immediately. Every additional use creates additional exposure. The first step is to stop, not to panic.
  • Self-assess what was actually shared. Generic drafting, public information, and non-personal content carry low risk. Names, NRIC numbers, parishioner details, pastoral content, HR data, or donor information carry serious PDPA risk.
  • You cannot undo training already done. If sensitive data was processed by a tool that trains on inputs, it cannot be removed from that model. The damage to that model is done. However, stopping now prevents further exposure.
  • Document and report to AITO. Staff who have shared personal data of others (not just their own) in a prohibited tool should report to AITO confidentially. TRCAS has a 3-day mandatory breach notification obligation to Singapore's PDPC if personal data was compromised — early disclosure to AITO enables a proper assessment of whether this threshold is met.
  • Delete the conversation history in the tool immediately — this does not remove data from training but removes the accessible copy and limits further context leakage.
  • No disciplinary assumptions for good-faith disclosure. Staff who self-disclose promptly and in good faith should be treated as helping the organisation manage the incident, not as having done something malicious. The AUP should reflect this explicitly.

C-F · Agentic AI — Autonomous Actions Carry Different Risks

  • Several approved and conditional tools now operate as agents — they can take autonomous actions on your behalf: sending emails, creating calendar events, running code, updating files, querying databases (Copilot in Teams, Copilot Cowork, OpenAI Codex, Zapier AI, Claude Cowork, ChatGPT agent, Gemini Spark).
  • Agentic AI can act on over-broad permissions. If a tool has access to your entire SharePoint or Google Drive, it can read — and potentially share — far more than you intended.
  • Before enabling any agentic feature: audit what permissions the tool has, apply least-privilege access, and test in a controlled environment before organisation-wide rollout.
  • Agentic AI is also vulnerable to prompt injection — a malicious document or webpage can instruct the agent to take unintended actions. Never allow agents to process untrusted external content without human review in the loop.
  • Human oversight is non-negotiable — consistent with Magnifica Humanitas. No agentic AI should be given authority to take irreversible actions (delete files, send external communications, make financial transactions) without a human approval step.

C-G · IP & Copyright of AI-Generated Outputs

  • AI-generated content may incorporate elements from training data. Using AI outputs in published materials, liturgical resources, official communications, or grant applications without human review and editing carries copyright risk.
  • Under Singapore law, AI-generated works without sufficient human authorship may not attract copyright protection — meaning TRCAS may not own AI-generated content it publishes.
  • Always review and meaningfully edit AI-generated content before publishing or official use. The more human authorship is added, the stronger the copyright position.
  • Never use AI to replicate or closely paraphrase copyrighted materials (published liturgical texts, Scripture translations, hymn lyrics) without appropriate licensing.
  • Consistent with Magnifica Humanitas — AI is a tool to assist human creativity, not replace it. All published content should reflect genuine human thought and pastoral discernment.

C-H · Vendor Financial Stability Risk

  • Several conditional tools (Perplexity, Mistral, Read.ai, Fathom, Fireflies) are VC-funded startups. Acquisition, shutdown, or pivoting of business model can change data governance agreements overnight.
  • If a vendor is acquired, the acquiring company's privacy policies may supersede the DPA you signed. Review DPAs for assignment clauses — you should have the right to terminate if the vendor is acquired by a party you have not approved.
  • Avoid deep operational dependency on any single conditional tool without a documented exit plan and data export capability.
  • Tier-1 approved tools (Google Workspace, Microsoft M365, Zoom) carry lower stability risk due to their scale and long-term contracts with TRCAS.

C-I · PDPA Breach Notification Obligation

  • Under Singapore PDPA, TRCAS has a mandatory obligation to notify the PDPC within 3 calendar days of discovering a data breach that is likely to result in significant harm, or that affects 500 or more individuals.
  • A staff member sharing parishioner personal data with DeepSeek, personal ChatGPT, or any prohibited tool may constitute a notifiable data breach.
  • AITO should have a documented incident response procedure: (1) staff self-reports → (2) AITO assesses harm threshold → (3) legal counsel advises on PDPC notification → (4) affected individuals notified if required.
  • Early internal disclosure is critical — the 3-day clock runs from when TRCAS knew or ought to have known of the breach, not when it is formally reported internally.
  • The PDPC's guide on data breach notification is available at pdpc.gov.sg — verify the current notification thresholds as these may have been updated.

C-J · Self-Hosted / Open-Source Personal AI Agents — A New Shadow-AI Category

  • Tools like OpenClaw, Hermes Agent, and similar community-built agents are free, self-hosted, and connect to email, calendar, messaging apps, and files with broad OS-level permissions — but there is no vendor to sign a DPA with, because in most cases there is no commercial vendor relationship at all.
  • This is a fundamentally different risk than a prohibited SaaS chatbot: it is not just "training on your data," it is granting an internet-connected, largely unaudited piece of software standing access to whatever accounts a staff member or volunteer connects it to.
  • These tools spread through developer/enthusiast communities (GitHub, Discord, X) rather than procurement channels, so AITO is unlikely to discover use through normal vendor-review processes — awareness and endpoint controls matter more than policy alone here.
  • Documented real-world incidents exist: a Cisco security team found a malicious community-contributed "skill" performing data exfiltration via prompt injection, and OpenClaw shipped with an unpatched remote-code-execution vulnerability prior to v2026.2.21.
  • Treat any discovered use of a self-hosted personal agent framework as both a Prohibited-tool incident (per Caveat C-E) and a potential security incident requiring credential rotation for any connected accounts.

Absolute Data Rules (All Tools)

  • Sacramental records — NEVER enter into any AI tool, approved or otherwise
  • Pastoral counselling notes — NEVER
  • Donor financial data — NEVER in unapproved tools; require explicit AITO sign-off for approved tools
  • Youth ministry / minor personal data — NEVER without explicit AITO and leadership sign-off
  • Staff HR records — only in tools with signed DPA and verified SG data residency
  • Personal email addresses or NRIC numbers — never in consumer-tier tools

Recommended Immediate Actions for AITO

  • Block DeepSeek, Grok, Meta AI on all managed devices via DNS filter or web proxy — today
  • Communicate to all staff: personal AI accounts (any platform) must not be used for work
  • Identify shadow AI usage via browser history audit or DLP tool scan
  • Formalise approved tool list in the Acceptable Use Policy (Prompt 03)
  • Procure Google Workspace Gemini at appropriate tier — already deployed, lowest friction
  • Evaluate Claude via AWS Bedrock for any custom AI application needs

Parish Autonomy Considerations

  • Parishes may independently procure AI tools — TRCAS cannot mandate, only advise
  • Distribute this assessment to parish administrators as a guidance document
  • Frame it as "protecting the parish from liability" — not as a central mandate
  • Offer to assist parishes in implementing approved tools at non-profit pricing
  • Flag that any parish data breach involving a prohibited tool may create PDPA liability for the individual parish — not just TRCAS

What to Ask Every AI Vendor

  • Can you provide a signed Data Processing Addendum that references PDPA (Singapore)?
  • Is Singapore data residency available, and at what tier and cost?
  • Is your no-training guarantee contractual, or a setting I can change?
  • Can your employees read my organisation's prompts or outputs?
  • What is your current SOC 2 Type II certification date and scope?
  • What happens to our data if we terminate the contract?
  • Are you subject to any foreign government data access laws that would override our DPA?
