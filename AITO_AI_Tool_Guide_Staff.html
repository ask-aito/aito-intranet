Section 1
Why This Matters

AI tools are powerful and genuinely useful for many everyday work tasks. But they also carry risks that are easy to miss — especially around privacy and the protection of sensitive information entrusted to us as a Church.

Our Responsibility as a Church Parishioners, donors, and staff trust TRCAS with personal and sometimes deeply sensitive information. That trust is sacred. When we use AI tools, we must ensure that trust is never broken — accidentally or otherwise. This guide helps you use AI tools confidently while protecting the people we serve.

Here is the core problem with many AI tools: when you type something into an AI chatbot on a personal or consumer account, that information often leaves your device and is stored on the AI company's servers — sometimes for years, sometimes used to train future AI models, and sometimes accessible to company employees for review.

This is why we cannot treat all AI tools equally. Some have strong enterprise agreements that protect our data. Others offer no protection at all. The difference is not always obvious from the product name or price — a paid personal subscription (eg. ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro) does not automatically give you enterprise-level privacy protection.

The Simple Rule If you are using a personal account — whether free or paid — it is almost certainly NOT safe for Archdiocesan work data. Always use the approved tools your organisation has set up, accessed through your @catholic.org.sg account.
Three Things Everyone Should Know

1. Paying more for a personal subscription does not make it safer for work. A paid ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, or Gemini Advanced personal account has almost the same privacy terms as the free version. The only meaningful protection is an organisational enterprise account with a signed data agreement — not the subscription price.

2. Deleting data from an AI tool does not remove it from AI models already trained on it. If sensitive information was used in training before you or AITO opted out, it cannot be taken back. This is why we must use only approved tools from the start — not after something goes wrong.

3. Even US-based approved tools (like Microsoft and Google) are subject to US government legal requests. Enterprise agreements reduce this risk significantly, but they do not eliminate it entirely. This is one reason why the most sensitive data — sacramental records, pastoral notes, donor data — must never enter any AI tool regardless of approval status.

Why DeepSeek Is Especially Dangerous DeepSeek is a free AI tool that may look similar to ChatGPT. It must never be used for any work purpose. Security researchers discovered in January 2025 that DeepSeek had a publicly accessible database containing over one million lines of user chat history, including private conversations. All data is stored on servers in China and is subject to Chinese government access laws. South Korea's data protection authority also found that DeepSeek was illegally transferring user data to a ByteDance-affiliated company. Multiple governments have banned it. Do not install the app on work devices or use it for any work-related matter.
Section 2
What You Must Never Share With Any AI Tool

Regardless of whether a tool is approved, conditional, or prohibited — the following categories of information must never be typed, pasted, uploaded, or discussed with any AI tool without explicit written approval from AITO.

Absolute Prohibition — No Exceptions These data types must never enter any AI tool under any circumstances. If you are unsure whether something falls into these categories, do not enter it — contact AITO first.
Data Type Examples Why It Must Be Protected
Sacramental Records Baptism, confirmation, marriage, annulment records; RCIA participant details Deeply personal religious data. Sharing this violates the dignity of the sacraments and Singapore PDPA.
Pastoral Counselling Notes Notes from confessional counselling, spiritual direction, family or personal crisis support Protected by both pastoral confidentiality and PDPA. Must never be disclosed to any third party, including AI systems.
Donor Financial Data Donation amounts, bank details, pledges, financial difficulties shared in confidence Protected by PDPA. Donors shared this information in trust — it must not be processed by external AI systems.
Minor / Youth Personal Data Names, contacts, school details, photos of anyone under 18 (youth ministry, catechism, scouts) Singapore PDPA applies enhanced protections to personal data of minors. Strict parental consent requirements apply.
Staff HR & Employment Data Salaries, performance appraisals, disciplinary records, medical certificates, personal grievances Employment data is sensitive personal data under PDPA. Must remain within HR systems only.
NRIC / Passport / ID Numbers Singapore NRIC numbers, passport numbers, FIN numbers of any individual These are high-value identity data under PDPA. Exposure creates serious risk of identity fraud.
Passwords & Access Credentials Passwords, API keys, system login details, security codes Never enter any credentials into any AI tool — approved or otherwise.
Crisis & Conflict Matters Safeguarding allegations, legal disputes, staff grievances, sensitive community conflicts AI must not be used to draft responses to these matters. They require human judgement and appropriate professional or legal advice.
A Note on AI-Generated Imagery AI-generated images for communications or promotions must be clearly artistic in nature. They must not depict real, identifiable people or events as if photographic, and must never be used to impersonate any clergy, staff member, or other real person. This applies across all approved image generation tools.
A Note on Meeting AI Tools For AI tools that record or transcribe meetings (Zoom AI, Google Meet notes, Fathom, Fireflies): never use these for pastoral counselling sessions, HR meetings involving personal staff matters, or any meeting where sensitive personal data will be discussed. All meeting participants must be informed that AI transcription is active before the meeting begins.

Section 4
How to Use AI Tools Safely

Even with approved tools, good habits matter. Here are the key principles to follow whenever you use any AI tool for work.

  1. Always use your @catholic.org.sg account. Never access AI tools through a personal Gmail, Microsoft, or other personal account when doing Archdiocesan work. The enterprise privacy protections only apply to our organisational accounts.

  2. Think before you paste. Before copying any content into an AI tool, ask yourself: does this contain any of the protected data categories in Section 2? If yes — do not paste it. If unsure — do not paste it.

  3. For meeting AI tools — always get consent first. Inform all participants at the start of every meeting that AI notes or transcription are active. Do not start AI transcription without telling everyone in the meeting.

  4. Personal subscriptions are not work subscriptions. If you personally pay for ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, or any other AI tool — those are personal consumer accounts. They do not carry the same protections as organisationally-managed accounts. Keep work content out of personal AI tools.

  5. AI can make mistakes. Always review AI-generated content before sending, publishing, or acting on it. AI tools can produce inaccurate, outdated, or inappropriate content. You are responsible for what you send — not the AI.

  6. When in doubt, leave it out. If you are unsure whether a piece of information is safe to enter into an AI tool — do not enter it. Contact AITO for guidance.

  7. Do not share AI outputs containing personal data. If an AI tool generates content that includes personal details about real people (names, contact details, health information), do not share that output broadly or store it in unsecured locations.

  8. Report unexpected behaviour. If an AI tool produces output that seems to have accessed information it shouldn't have, or if you accidentally enter sensitive data into a tool, inform AITO immediately. Early reporting limits harm.

  9. Keep agentic features off. Do not enable connector, agent, or auto-join "notetaker" features in any AI tool, and do not connect AI tools to each other or to Archdiocesan accounts or systems, without explicit AITO approval. When in doubt, leave these features disabled.

  10. Only use AITO-approved tools to record or transcribe meetings. Do not use unapproved transcription apps or recording tools — including apps that save audio or transcripts to personal device storage or a personal cloud account — for any Archdiocesan meeting. If you use a hardware recording device (such as a dedicated AI recorder), check with AITO first before using it for any work meeting — specific conditions apply. Recordings and transcripts must be held only in approved Archdiocesan systems with appropriate access controls, and deleted when no longer needed. Use Zoom AI Companion or Fathom for meeting transcription unless AITO has approved an alternative.

AI-Enabled Fraud and Voice-Cloning Scams Be alert to AI-driven phishing and voice-cloning scams. Criminals can now generate convincing audio or video impersonating clergy, leadership, or colleagues to request urgent fund transfers or the release of sensitive information. If you receive any unexpected or urgent request to transfer money or share sensitive data — even if it appears to come from a known person — independently verify it through a separate trusted channel (e.g. call back on a known number) before acting.
Remember — AI Is a Tool, Not a Replacement for Judgement AI tools can help us work more effectively and serve the community better. But they work best when used thoughtfully. Apply the same care and discernment to AI that you would to any tool you use in your ministry or work.
If You Have Already Been Using a Prohibited Tool — What To Do

First: do not panic. Many staff discover they have been using a prohibited tool without realising it. Here is what to do:

  1. Stop using it immediately. Every additional conversation creates more exposure.
  2. Delete your conversation history in that tool. This does not undo any AI training that may have already happened, but it removes the accessible copy and limits further risk.
  3. Think about what you shared. Did you share any personal information about parishioners, staff, or donors? If yes, proceed to step 4. If you only used it for generic tasks with no personal data, the risk is low — but still stop using it.
  4. Report to AITO promptly and confidentially. If you shared personal data of others, AITO needs to know so they can assess whether there is a formal reporting obligation. There is no penalty for honest, prompt disclosure — in fact, prompt reporting is the responsible thing to do and is treated as such.
  5. Switch to an approved alternative. AITO can help you find an approved tool that meets your needs.

Note: once data has been used to train an AI model, it cannot be "removed" from that model. This is why using approved tools from the start is so important — but for past use, the focus should be on stopping further exposure and disclosing appropriately.

Two More Things Worth Knowing

AI tools that act on your behalf (agentic AI). Some AI tools — like Copilot in Teams, AI assistants in email, or automation tools like Zapier — can now take actions for you: send emails, create files, update records. These are powerful but carry extra risk. If the AI has access to more than it needs, or if it misunderstands an instruction, it can share or act on data you did not intend. Before enabling these features, check with AITO what the tool has access to. Always keep a human review step before any AI takes a significant action on your behalf.

AI-generated content and copyright. Content written by AI tools may not be automatically owned by TRCAS under Singapore law, and may incorporate material from the AI's training data. Always review and meaningfully edit AI-generated content before publishing it officially — in newsletters, on the website, in liturgical materials, or in official correspondence. AI is a drafting assistant, not an author. The pastoral voice and judgement must always be yours.

Section 5
Questions & Support

If you have questions about whether a specific AI tool or use case is acceptable, or if you want to request access to an approved tool, contact AITO through the usual IT support channels.

Common Questions

Can I use AI tools for my parish communications?
Yes — approved tools like Gemini in Workspace and Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat are suitable for drafting newsletters, announcements, and general parish communications. Do not include personal data of parishioners in your prompts.

Can I use AI for my own personal productivity (not work content)?
Your personal use of AI tools on personal devices for non-work content is your own choice. This guide only covers the use of AI tools with Archdiocesan work content.

What if a tool I use regularly is not on this list?
Contact AITO. Do not assume a tool is safe because it is not listed here. AITO will assess it and update this guide accordingly.

I accidentally pasted sensitive data into an AI tool. What should I do?
Contact AITO immediately. Do not wait. Delete the conversation if you can. Early notification allows AITO to assess the situation and determine whether there is a formal reporting obligation to Singapore's data protection authority (PDPC). There is no penalty for honest, prompt disclosure.

I have been using a prohibited tool — what now?
Stop using it and refer to the guidance in Section 4 above. Report to AITO confidentially. Prompt, honest disclosure is always the right course of action and will be treated constructively.

Key Terms

Plain-English definitions of technical terms used in this guide and in related AITO documents.

Term What It Means in Plain English
Agent-to-Agent Arrangements where one AI system instructs or acts on another AI system without a human directing each step. Not permitted at TRCAS without exceptional approval from AITO and the DPO.
Agentic AI AI that can plan and carry out multi-step tasks on its own, with limited human supervision — for example, reading your emails, creating a document, and sending a reply, all without you clicking each step. Copilot Cowork and Claude Cowork are examples. Extra caution required — always check with AITO before enabling.
Agentic Tool Use / MCP Configurations that give an AI tool the ability to connect to and act within external accounts, databases, or systems — for example, via the Model Context Protocol. Also includes "notetaker" bots that auto-join meetings and take actions on your behalf. Not permitted at TRCAS without explicit AITO approval.
Approved (Enterprise) AI Tool A tool that AITO has reviewed and authorised under an organisational account or agreement that provides appropriate security, confidentiality, and data protection terms — including a commitment not to train on our data. Only these tools may be used for Archdiocesan work.
LLM Assistant A Large Language Model assistant — a general-purpose AI tool you type questions or instructions into and it generates text in response. Examples: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot.
Public AI Tool A consumer or free AI service not covered by a TRCAS organisational agreement — where your inputs may be stored or used to train the provider's AI models. Examples: ChatGPT Free/Plus, Claude Free/Pro, Gemini personal app. Must not be used for any Archdiocesan work content.
